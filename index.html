The check

Will you be able to work on that flight?

Type the flight number and you get three things: the odds it draws next-gen WiFi, what is on board when it does not, and how far to lean on both. Every figure on this site names its source and its date. No account, nothing stored. The page runs in two halves — the record first, then the companion, a Chrome extension that carries the same odds onto the page where you book.

Any flight number works: UA212, AS15, AA1234. An airline name works too. The answer comes from our own daily-verified data and the card names the method it used. No account, nothing stored.

The live check needs JavaScript. Pick your airline instead. Every page below carries the same ConnectScore, the method behind it, and how much to trust it.

The tier column is how the row was derived: A resolves your aircraft to a registration, B stops at the aircraft type, C is the airline’s own published fleet count and D is a signed deal with nothing in the air. ConnectScore is an expected value over a whole fleet, not a prediction about one flight: United measured 320, 56 and 15 Mbps on three systems in one livery in one reporting period. Aircraft whose system an airline does not publish are left out of the denominator rather than assumed. Signed-but-unflown deals (AA Starlink 2027, DL/B6 Amazon Leo) score zero until they fly.

The one pitch on this site

A badge on every flight in the results

WiFi Odds for Flights is a Chrome extension, free in the Chrome Web Store under that name. On a united.com or app.navan.com results page every flight picks up an odds badge, one click re-sorts the page by odds while prices and times stay put, and a panel ranks the route’s departures the way the answer card above does. No account and no tracking, there or here. The pictures are captures of the shipped build.

Real screenshots · v1.5.1united.com · app.navan.com
  1. 1Search

    You search united.com the way you always do. The extension adds nothing to the page until there are results on it, and it never touches the booking or the payment steps.

  2. 2Odds

    Every result picks up its own badge: how often that flight number has actually drawn a Starlink aircraft. 49% here, 44% on the next one. Once the assigned tail is confirmed equipped the badge shows a ✓ instead of a percentage.

  3. 3Sort

    One click reorders the whole page best-odds-first, prices and times untouched, so you can see what the good connection actually costs you. Same panel on Navan as on united.com.

  4. 4Guard

    The panel names what it cannot know yet: tails firm up around 48 hours out. Watching that window after you book is the Tail-swap Guardian, which is built, in test, and ships in 2.1.

Captured on the real united.com and Navan with the store build. Nothing here is a mockup. The percentages in the badges are the odds the extension actually computed for that route.
