The trust layer

A number should show its work.

WiFi Odds separates what is flying, what was announced, and what nobody has published. The answer begins with the question you are asking.

Method effective 28 Jul 2026
01 / Two questions

Do not make one number do two jobs.

Next-gen odds lead the site because they answer the original travel question. ConnectScore remains useful, but it describes the entire fleet you can encounter today.

PRIMARY · DEFAULT SORT

Will I draw next-gen WiFi?

The chance of drawing a Starlink or Amazon Leo aircraft when no aircraft has been assigned yet. It is a fleet probability, not a promise about a seat, route, or departure.

Next-gen odds
SUPPORTING · WHOLE FLEET

How good is the WiFi flying today?

ConnectScore is a 0–100 rating across the whole published fleet. It blends each fitted system by fleet share, observed quality, and whether the passenger can use it free.

02 / The non-negotiable rule

Unknown is a state. It is not zero.

A real zero requires a published denominator and a published absence of next-gen aircraft. Silence from an airline cannot be converted into a sourced-looking number.

Unpublished rows stay neutral and unranked.

They are excluded from the next-gen denominator and never placed at the bottom as zeros. When a carrier publishes the missing count, the row becomes numeric at the next build.

03 / Confirmed floors

Publish what is known. Stop there.

For “streaming or better,” some fleets have a confirmed equipped share and a remainder whose system is genuinely unknown. We publish the confirmed share as a floor. We do not blend the unknown remainder into a guess.

The greater-than-or-equal sign means the final share can rise when the missing fleet detail arrives. It cannot fall below what the sources already confirm.

Read “≥” as: at least this much is confirmed. The rest is unreported, not absent.
04 / Evidence vocabulary

Every claim says what kind of evidence it is.

These labels are not decoration. They tell you whether a figure came from observed performance, a public fleet statement, or a supplier describing its own product.

Measured

Somebody observed it.

A speed test, latency trace, flight record, or other result gathered from service in use. Measurements keep their sample and date because one session cannot stand in for a fleet.

Performance evidence · source and observation date required
Reported

A named source published it.

Aircraft counts, rollout dates, service terms, and per-tail records from airlines, trackers, filings, or dated reporting. This is the main evidence class behind fleet odds.

Fleet evidence · publisher and effective date required
Vendor claim

The supplier says it can.

A capability or specification published by the company selling the system. Useful context, never presented as passenger-measured performance.

Product claim · vendor and announcement date required
05 / The arithmetic

Simple formulas. Strict inputs.

The model is intentionally legible. Most of the work is deciding which facts are licensed to enter it, and refusing to fill a missing field with an assumption.

Next-gen odds

confirmed next-gen aircraft
÷ aircraft with a published system

Only Starlink and Amazon Leo enter the numerator. Airlines with an unpublished equipped count remain non-numeric.

ConnectScore

fleet share × system quality
× free-for-you factor

Each published fleet segment contributes its weighted points. The rows sum to a 0–100 whole-fleet rating. It answers quality today, not the chance of next-gen.
