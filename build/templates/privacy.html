The short version

No accounts. No analytics. No advertising. No cookies. Nothing about you is sold or shared. The extension's only outbound calls are anonymous route and flight-number lookups to two public community trackers. The one thing this site ever writes to your browser is your light or dark preference, under the key woTheme.

§1 · The product

What the extension does

It shows the historical probability that a given flight is operated by an aircraft carrying modern high-speed satellite wifi, meaning Starlink or Amazon Leo, rather than legacy service. The odds appear as colour-coded badges on airline search results and as a ConnectScore in the popup.

§2 · Collection

Nothing personal is collected

The extension and this website do not collect, store, transmit, sell or share personal information. There are no analytics, no tracking pixels, no accounts, no sign-in, and no server of ours that receives your data. The list below is the complete set of things that happen.

  • Reading pages, locally. On a flight search at united.com, alaskaair.com or Navan, the extension reads the routes and flight numbers already displayed on the page in front of you. That parsing happens in your browser. Nothing about the page is uploaded.
  • Odds lookups. To attach odds to a flight the extension asks the public APIs of the independent community trackers unitedstarlinktracker.com and alaskastarlinktracker.com. Each request carries a route such as DEN to SFO, or a flight number and a date. No booking details, no passenger names, no fare or payment data, nothing that identifies you.
  • Caching, locally. Responses are cached for about six hours in the browser's extension storage, next to a handful of interface preferences: panel collapsed, keep-sorted, and whether you turned Alaska support on. Clearing that storage or uninstalling removes all of it.
  • Selector updates. Roughly once a day the extension fetches a small configuration file from wifiodds.com that says where the relevant elements sit on the airline pages, so a site redesign does not break it. That request carries nothing about you beyond what any web request reveals to a host, which is your IP address. It is not logged and not joined to anything.
§3 · Intake

Field reports you send us

This site has one form. It sits on the methodology page, it posts to /api/report, and it asks what you flew, when you flew it, and what your speed test read. There is no account step and no email field, so a report is the only thing that arrives.

Something has to stop one connection filing a thousand rows, and a captcha cannot do it here: Turnstile, reCAPTCHA and hCaptcha are all third-party scripts, which this site does not load. The cap is server-side instead. Your network address goes into a SHA-256 digest along with a secret salt and the current UTC hour, and the digest is what gets stored. The address itself is hashed in memory. It is never written down, never logged and never sent back to you.

Because the hour is part of the input, the digest changes on the hour. Two hours of submissions from one connection cannot be joined to each other. Five reports per digest per hour is the limit. No cookie is set and nothing is measured about your browser.

Every submission lands unpublished and a person reads it before it appears on the site. The only thing about you that can ever be published is the name or handle you type into the credit box, and that box can be left empty.

§5 · The website

What wifiodds.com does

wifiodds.com is a static site on Cloudflare Pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics and embeds no third-party script. Every page is prerendered, so the pages hold their numbers rather than fetching them. The fonts are served from this origin. Cloudflare processes standard short-lived request logs as the hosting provider, and we neither read nor keep them.

§6 · Children

Data from children

The extension and the site are general-audience tools that collect no personal data from anyone, so they collect none from children.
