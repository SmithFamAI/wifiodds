What it does, in the order it does it.

A united.com result list, from the moment it loads to the moment it is sorted.

united.com · DEN → SFO

Sorted by historical next-gen odds · Keep site order

  1. You search. The booking site renders its own list, in its own order.
  2. It notices. The panel opens and announces that it is checking, once.
  3. Odds appear. Each row gets a labelled figure, or a named reason it has none.
  4. It decides. Two scored flights, an 8-point lead, medium confidence: it names one.
  5. It sorts, and you can undo. Rows move, the panel says so, and one control puts them back.

Every figure above already appears elsewhere on this page and comes from the same captured united.com data. UA 2265 at 53% is the arithmetic of the decision card's own sentence, “11 points higher historical odds than UA2265”, against UA 1812 at 64%. This is a composition of states the product renders, not a new capture, and the fares are illustrative.

3 · Automatic sorting, and a real undo

On single-carrier sites every row is the same airline, so ordering by odds displaces nobody. Sorting is on by default there. Press the button to watch the page reorder, then take it back. "Keep site order" restores the booking site's own sequence exactly.

On Navan, where several carriers share one list, this is off by default. Moving a scored United flight up necessarily moves an unscored airline down, and an airline we have no data for is unknown, not worse. There you get an explicit action and a coverage line instead. Google Flights is never reordered at all, whichever option you pick: its results list is virtualised and Google owns its own ordering, so the extension only adds labelled chips there.

Coverage: United. Other airlines stay unscored and keep the booking site's order.

5 · What the numbers mean

These are two different questions, never merged into one leaderboard.

Next-gen odds

The chance this flight draws a Starlink or Amazon Leo aircraft. Per-flight history exists for United and Alaska only, 2 of 18 airlines. For the rest, the row shows fleet share and says that is what it is.

Streaming-class ConnectScore

How good the airline's system is today, published as a floor across the whole fleet. United 42, Alaska 55, Southwest 16. Unresolved aircraft never count as zero.
