02 · How it works

How the extension labels and sorts flights.

01

Reads the flight number

The extension uses flight numbers shown on the page. It doesn’t read your account, email, fare, or browsing history.

02

Checks public history

United and Alaska flights can get per-flight next-gen WiFi history from public trackers.

03

Adds a clear label

Each number says whether it describes that flight or the airline as a whole.

04

Sorts where undo works

Supported United and Alaska pages use automatic sorting. Keep site order puts each row back.

The two measures answer different questions. Next-Gen estimates the chance that a flight uses a Starlink or Amazon Leo aircraft. The WiFi Odds site’s Streaming score rates an airline’s WiFi across its whole fleet. The current extension labels are listed in the release notes below.

08 · Install and privacy

Install and site-access requirements.

Site access

United and Navan work as soon as you install the extension. Alaska and Google Flights stay off until you choose to grant access. There’s no email, login, or remote code.

Where the numbers come from

Per-flight history comes from unitedstarlinktracker.com and alaskastarlinktracker.com. Airline-wide scores keep their own source dates, and missing data stays unknown.
