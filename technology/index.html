What the booking page hides

Three eras. One WiFi label.

“WiFi available” can mean a video call, a movie, or a loading spinner. The difference is the system on the aircraft, not the logo painted on it.

Technology map · 28 Jul 2026
01 / The three tiers

What it feels like in the seat.

These are passenger experience tiers, not advertised peak speeds. Aircraft assignments move, cabin load changes, and a system name is more useful than one best-case number.

1
Next-gen · low-earth orbit

Internet that happens to be on an airplane.

Starlink is the system flying today. Amazon Leo is the other next-gen network carriers have signed for. The short signal path is built for responsive work, streaming, and live conversation.

Passenger experience
  • Live calls can feel conversational
  • Streaming and large downloads are realistic
  • Cloud work behaves more like ground internet
Reported · carrier and provider announcements · Jul 2026 · Amazon Leo has no passenger measurement yet
2
Streaming-class · modern GEO

Working WiFi, with the lag.

Modern geostationary systems can carry real work and entertainment. The connection can move plenty of data while still pausing the back-and-forth of a live conversation.

Passenger experience
  • Movies and music can work well
  • Email, documents, and uploads are practical
  • Live calls are less forgiving of the delay
Reported · carrier system disclosures and product records · Jul 2026
3
Gen 1 · legacy ATG and early satellite

Dial-up in the air.

The badge says WiFi. The experience is closer to a shared modem: messaging, email, and patient page loads. Modern websites can overwhelm it before the cabin fills.

Passenger experience
  • Messaging is the safest expectation
  • Browsing may need patience
  • Streaming and live calls are fragile
Reported · carrier fleet disclosures and current system ledgers · Jul 2026
02 / The gap that matters

Tier two and tier three look identical while booking.

Both can appear as “WiFi available.” One is a useful modern connection. The other may only keep a chat thread alive. That hidden gap changes which flight is worth taking.

04 / Why it feels different

Distance shows up as delay.

Low-earth-orbit satellites pass much closer to the aircraft than a geostationary satellite. The signal has less distance to travel, so two-way actions can respond sooner.

A modern GEO system can still move enough data for streaming. What distance cannot hide is the pause in the conversation: request, wait, response.

Orbit is not the whole product. The onboard router, purchased capacity, cabin load, ground network, and traffic management can still change the experience.

05 / What we refuse to fake

There is no honest universal speed for a tier.

A plausible range without a publisher is still invented. Where the evidence cannot support a number, the page names the unknown instead.

The missing facts are part of the answer.

Amazon Leo has no passenger measurement because no carrier is flying it in passenger service yet. Cabin load is not published. Aircraft routers are rarely visible while booking. A technology label narrows the likely experience, but it cannot erase those variables.
