What the booking page hides

Three eras. One WiFi label.

“WiFi available” can mean a movie that plays on the first tap, a download that crawls, or a spinner that never resolves. What changes is the system bolted to the aircraft, not the logo painted on it.

Technology map · 28 Jul 2026
01 / The three tiers

What it feels like in the seat.

These are passenger-experience tiers, not advertised peak speeds. Aircraft move between fleets, cabins fill up, and the name of the system tells you more than one best-case number ever will. The meters below are relative, not measured throughput.

1
Next-gen · low-orbit satellite

Internet that happens to be on a plane.

Starlink is the low-orbit system flying in passenger service today. Amazon Leo is the other low-orbit network carriers have signed for. A short signal path keeps streaming, big downloads, and interactive work close to what they feel like on the ground.

Tier two and tier three look identical while booking.

Both show up as “WiFi available.” One is a capable modern connection. The other may only keep a chat thread alive. That hidden gap decides which flight is worth taking.

04 / Why it feels different

Distance shows up as delay.

A low-orbit satellite passes far closer to the aircraft than a geostationary one. The signal has less distance to cover, so two-way actions come back sooner. Both Starlink and Amazon Leo are low-orbit systems, so the split here is orbit height, not brand.

A modern geostationary system still moves enough data for streaming. What the distance cannot hide is the pause on every round trip: request, wait, response.

Orbit is not the whole product. The onboard router, the capacity the airline bought, the cabin load, the ground network, and traffic shaping can each move the real experience.

05 / The unknowns

No single speed describes a tier.

A range with no publisher behind it is a guess. Where the evidence cannot carry a number, the page names the unknown and stops there.

The missing facts are part of the answer.

Amazon Leo has no passenger measurement because no carrier is flying it in passenger service yet. Cabin load is not published. The router on your tail is rarely visible while booking. A technology label narrows the likely experience; it cannot erase those variables.
